One restaurant, two branches,
one website

The address Li’s uses on the web today names Nairobi. Nyali is the original restaurant and keeps the longer week, and a guest who arrives at that address has no particular reason to think the coast is included at all.

The answer to that is not a second website. It is one site that carries both restaurants properly. Here is the reasoning, plainly:

  • Two sites compete with each other in Google. Search standing is earned per domain. Two sites split whatever Li’s builds between them, and because the menu pages would be near-identical, Google reads each as a duplicate of the other. One domain carrying both restaurants concentrates that strength instead of halving it.
  • One site can still win both cities. A dedicated page for each restaurant — its own address, its own hours, its own phone number, its own photographs — is how a two-branch business ranks locally in two places. You get the Nyali search and the Gigiri search from a single domain.
  • Guests choose the restaurant, then the branch. Nobody searches for a branch. They decide where they want to eat, and only then work out which one is nearer. Two sites force that second decision before the first one has been made.
  • Half the upkeep, permanently. One menu to keep accurate, one plan, one set of hours. Two sites means every price change is made twice, forever — and in practice the second one quietly drifts out of date.
WHEN TWO SITES WOULD BE THE RIGHT CALL

If one branch traded under a different name, served a different menu, or were separately owned, it would need its own site and its own identity. That is not the case here — one name, one kitchen tradition, two rooms.

The menu is the website

For a restaurant, the menu is not one page among several. It is the reason anybody visits the site at all.

Most restaurant websites in Kenya publish theirs as a PDF, or as a photograph of the printed card. Three things follow from that, and each one costs covers:

  • Google cannot read it. A dish inside a PDF or an image is invisible to search. Someone typing “peking duck Nyali” or “dim sum Gigiri” into a phone will not find Li’s, because as far as Google is concerned those words are not on your website.
  • A phone cannot use it. A PDF on a phone means pinching and dragging around a page laid out for A4 paper. Most people give up and choose the restaurant whose menu they could actually read.
  • You cannot change a price without help. Every change means a new PDF, which means finding whoever made the last one. So the menu on the website slowly stops matching the menu on the table.

On this site the carte is the page. All 84 dishes across ten courses are real text, set the way a printed menu is set, and readable on a phone without zooming.

When a price moves, it is one line changed, and that is inside the monthly plan. When someone searches for a dish you are known for, there is a page that can answer them.

THE SAME APPLIES TO GOOGLE'S OWN LISTING

What Google shows beside a restaurant — hours, phone number, whether it is open now — is only as good as what it can read on the website it points at. Two restaurants with different hours and different numbers need two pages saying so plainly, or Google guesses, and a guest arrives at a closed door on a Monday.

How this is built,
and why it matters

Most websites sold in Kenya at any price are WordPress with a bought theme. They look like the thousands of other sites using that theme, they slow down as plugins accumulate, and they need constant patching to stay secure.

This one is written by hand — 14,622 lines of HTML, CSS and JavaScript across four pages, with no theme, no page builder and no framework underneath it. Three things follow from that, and all three are practical:

  • It cannot be mistaken for another restaurant’s site. The design exists nowhere else. For a restaurant whose competition is a row of near-identical template sites, that is not a cosmetic point.
  • It loads fast and stays fast. Every page is under half a megabyte, and it scrolls at a full sixty frames a second on a mid-range phone — both measured, not estimated. Speed is also a ranking factor, so it compounds.
  • There is nothing to hack. The site is served as plain files. The usual WordPress attack surface simply does not exist, which is also why the monthly plan does not carry a security line item.
